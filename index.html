Keeping each voice distinct

Pick a speaker to see how their voice was carried into French and Italian.

Kane

Confused, frightened, thinking out loud. His panic lives in the syntax, so I kept the broken rhythm and the trailing ellipses rather than tidying him into correct sentences.

Waking up on the train
EN

Where am I? I don't remember anything…

FR

IT

Dove sono? Non ricordo niente…

When it spills over
EN

Look, fuck… I don't even know what I'm doing on this train.

FR

Écoute, putain… je sais même pas ce que je fous dans ce train.

IT

Senti, cazzo… non so nemmeno che ci faccio su questo treno.

Why: the studio asked for a medium profanity level. "Putain" and "cazzo" are the natural spoken equivalents at that level, and the dropped "ne" in "je sais même pas" keeps Kane sounding rattled rather than written.

Phoebe

Calm, controlled, always a step ahead. Her lines are short and plain on purpose, so the work was resisting the urge to explain her.

Three words, three accusations
EN

Fear, guilt… or just habit.

FR

La peur, la culpabilité… ou juste l'habitude.

IT

Paura, colpa… o semplicemente abitudine.

Why: French needs the articles, and they add a slightly clinical distance that suits her. Italian drops them and stays as bare as the English.

Ray

Charming, easy-going, never quite answering the question. He should sound friendly while he steers the conversation away from himself.

Condescension, lightly
EN

Ah, sure. Poor you. Always the victim.

FR

Ah, bien sûr. Pauvre petit. Toujours la victime.

IT

Ah, certo. Poverino. Sempre la vittima.

Why: "Pauvre petit" and "Poverino" treat Kane like a child, which is exactly the power play in the English. A literal "pauvre toi" would just sound sympathetic.

Dr. Lookman

Polite, professional, and quietly wrong. Nothing he says is a threat on paper, which is what makes him unsettling.

Reassurance with a catch
EN

Don't worry, you're safe here… for now.

FR

Ne t'inquiète pas, tu es en sécurité ici… pour l'instant.

IT

Care that sounds like surveillance
EN

See you soon, I'll be watching you… Ray.

FR

À bientôt. Je te surveillerai… Ray.

IT

A presto. Ti terrò d'occhio… Ray.

Why: a real doctor would say "vous" or "Lei". I proposed both options; the studio chose "tu", because closeness from Lookman feels invasive in a way professional distance never could.

The posters

Posters on the train break the fourth wall and taunt the player directly. I delivered a milder default and a harder alternative for each, so the studio could set the intensity without a second pass. They went with the harder set.

Mocking the player for sticking around
EN

You're still here. How obedient… Spoiler: you're not getting any.

FR

T'es encore là. En manque de compliments ? Spoiler : t'en auras pas.

IT

Cold and final
EN

Time rewinds. I don't. Bye.

FR

Le temps rembobine. Pas moi. Salut.

IT

Il tempo riavvolge. Io no. Ciao.

Why: "rembobine" and "riavvolge" are the words for rewinding a tape, which keeps the game's loop feeling mechanical. The short fragments hit harder than any full sentence would.

Questions I raised, and what the studio chose

Where a line had more than one valid reading, I sent options with my reasoning instead of guessing.

Should Dr. Lookman use formal or informal address?
French and Italian force a choice English never has to make. Formal "vous" and "Lei" are realistic for a doctor; informal "tu" makes him feel closer and more invasive. Studio chose informal "tu" for everyone, Lookman included.
How specific should Ray's briefcase be?
"Mallette" and "valigetta" keep it ambiguous. Italian "ventiquattrore" would make it clearly an office briefcase and sharpen Ray's persona. Studio chose the ambiguous "mallette" and "valigetta".
Keep "loop" in English, or translate it?
Italian gamers use "loop" naturally and it keeps a meta, game-logic feel. In French, "boucle" is the natural word and avoids an anglicism. Studio agreed: "loop" in Italian, "boucle" in French.
How strong should the swearing be?
Without a confirmed age rating, I calibrated a middle level and marked where it could go softer or harder. Studio confirmed a medium level.
How do we handle a player whose gender isn't fixed?
I restructured lines to avoid gender agreement where possible, rewrote dynamic UI strings so any inserted item name still agrees, and kept UI conventional while saving the strangeness for the story. Approved as proposed.

The building side talks to you like a friend. The surveyor's dossier reads like a government file. Keeping that contrast was the heart of the job.

Second drafts

Every string got a review pass. These are the kinds of fixes that make the difference between accurate and right.

  1. Food Truck

    Furgone di CiboCamioncino del Cibo

    "Furgone" is a delivery van. "Camioncino" is small and a little affectionate, which suits a game about cramming charming clutter into tiny spaces.

  2. …census omissions and the undercounted

    non conteggiatipersone non censite

    English can turn an adjective into a group of people. Italian can't do that as comfortably mid-list, so the phrase needed a noun to carry the same weight.

Placeholders that survive Italian grammar

Text in curly brackets is filled in by the game. The rule was to never change what's inside, but I could move it.

{Owner}'s {Name} becomes {Name} di {Owner}

Italian has no apostrophe-s possessive, so the two placeholders swap places. I flagged it with a test path so the developer could confirm the engine handles the new order.

Also in the handoff

  • Place namesRecommended keeping fantasy compounds like Westmarsh and Crosswaters in English, with Italian alternatives ready if the developer preferred them.
  • Internal labelsFlagged strings like ShortLeft and StackedTemple that looked like code references, so nothing internal gets translated by mistake.
  • Source typosCaught four spelling slips in the English, including "purley" and "Miscelaneous", so the original could be fixed too.
  • Steam pageAdapted rather than translated: "a casual game through and through" became the more natural "al cento per cento".

How a project runs

The same steps on a 700-string indie game or a long narrative script.

  1. Play it firstBuild, brief, style guide. I want to see where every string appears before I touch it.
  2. Set the voicesRegister, character voices and key terms, agreed before the bulk of the work.
  3. Translate and reviewEvery string read again in context, narrative text read aloud.
  4. Send queriesOptions with reasoning for anything ambiguous, so the team decides quickly.
  5. Check and hand offPlaceholder and file validation, then a report the team can sign off.

If a literal translation loses the feeling of a line, the feeling wins.
